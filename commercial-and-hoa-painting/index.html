Boulder CO Painters provides commercial and HOA painting across Boulder County, covering retail and restaurant frontage, offices, flex and light industrial space, lab and research buildings, and community association exteriors. Commercial projects begin with a walkthrough, then a written scope covering surfaces, preparation, products, phasing and schedule, with after-hours and phased work so operations continue and residents stay informed.

Four kinds of commercial work, four different problems

Pearl Street and downtown retail

Historic frontage, constant pedestrian traffic, and businesses that cannot close. The work gets phased, scheduled around trading hours, and contained carefully because the sidewalk is three feet from the wall. Properties in the historic district also carry the same review requirements as residential landmarks.

Gunbarrel offices and flex space

Larger surfaces, easier access, and tenants who need their corridors and suites usable in the morning. Most of this work happens in evenings and over weekends, phased floor by floor or suite by suite.

Lab and research buildings

Boulder has an unusual concentration of research and tech tenancy, which brings requirements most commercial painters do not encounter: coating specifications tied to cleanability, scheduling around sensitive equipment, and documented product data for facilities records. Tell us the constraints at walkthrough and they go into the scope.

HOA and community associations

Phased exterior cycles across multiple buildings, color submissions prepared for the board, resident notification schedules so nobody is surprised by a ladder outside their bedroom, and documentation that supports reserve planning. The painting is the straightforward part. The coordination is the service.

How a commercial estimate is built

  1. Walkthrough first. Surfaces, substrate condition, access, staging and operating constraints.
  2. Written scope. Preparation by surface, product and sheen per substrate, coat counts, phases and schedule.
  3. Insurance and documentation. Certificate provided before anyone arrives, matched to whatever your property manager or board requires.
  4. Phasing plan. Which areas, in which order, during which hours.
  5. Single point of contact. One person who answers the phone for the duration.

Questions

Commercial and HOA questions from Boulder homeowners

What kinds of commercial painting do you handle in Boulder?
Retail and restaurant frontage, offices, flex and light industrial space, research and lab buildings, HOAs and multi-family residential, inside and out.
Can you work after hours?
Yes. Evening, overnight and weekend scheduling is standard on commercial projects, and most work is phased so only part of a property is affected at any time.
How do you handle HOA color approvals?
We prepare the samples and submission materials for the board, and we do it early. Approvals can take weeks in spring, and starting the paperwork late is the most common cause of a delayed community repaint.
Do you provide a certificate of insurance?
Yes, before any work begins. Send the specific requirements from your property manager, board or building owner along with the request and we will match them.
Can you paint a historic downtown storefront?
Yes, with the understanding that exterior changes in Boulder's historic districts go through review first. That timeline is built into the schedule rather than discovered partway through.
Is the warranty the same on commercial work?
Yes. Commercial and HOA projects carry the same written 5-year workmanship warranty as residential work.
