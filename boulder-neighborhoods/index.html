Inside the city

Eight Boulder neighborhoods, eight different jobs.

A Mapleton Hill Queen Anne and a Table Mesa ranch are both "a house in Boulder." They have almost nothing else in common.

  • Historic districts with Landmarks review
  • Mid-century stock with long trim runs
  • Student rentals on an academic calendar
  • Foothills exposure at the open-space edge

Boulder neighborhood by neighborhood

Mapleton Hill · 80304

Boulder's first fashionable address, laid out in the 1880s on the money that came out of the mines above town.

  • The neighborhood sits inside a designated historic district. Exterior color and material changes go to Landmarks review before any work starts, and the review is substantive rather than a rubber stamp.
  • Essentially the entire housing stock predates 1978, so the federal lead-safe rule governs how any painted surface gets disturbed, contained and cleaned.
  • Queen Anne turrets, dentil molding, bracketed eaves and original double-hung sash. A single facade here can take longer than an entire modern elevation.

Whittier · 80302

The blocks east of Broadway between Pearl and the hill, dense with 1890s cottages and brick two-flats.

  • Also a historic district, with the same review requirements and the same century of accumulated coatings to work through.
  • Alley access and shared lot lines make containment a real constraint. Masking matters more than equipment here.
  • A mix of owner-occupied homes and long-held rentals means two different conversations on the same block.

Newlands · 80304

North Boulder below Wonderland Lake, where 1950s ranches sit beside full scrapes and rebuilds.

  • Build years on a single block can span seventy years, so the preparation plan is set house by house rather than street by street.
  • Proximity to open space means more airborne dust and seed landing on wet film during spring and late summer.
  • Surviving original ranches still carry wood fascia and soffit that reward real scraping and priming over a quick recoat.

Table Mesa · 80305

The large mid-century neighborhood below NCAR, built mostly between 1958 and the mid-1970s.

  • Low horizontal rooflines with long uninterrupted fascia runs. The trim linear footage here surprises people.
  • Direct foothills exposure means hard afternoon sun plus chinook winds hitting the same west elevations.
  • Kitchens of the same vintage generally have solid cabinet boxes, which makes refinishing the highest-return project in the neighborhood.

Martin Acres · 80305

Compact post-war homes south of Baseline, Boulder's most affordable owner-occupied pocket.

  • Small footprints mean a complete exterior is often a two or three day project rather than a week.
  • Built almost entirely before 1978, so lead-safe practices are assumed from the start.
  • A meaningful share is investor-owned, which puts fast durable turnovers alongside full homeowner repaints.

University Hill · 80302

The dense blocks west of campus, where single-family homes became student housing decades ago.

  • Turnover work compresses into the weeks between academic leases, and the calendar fills months ahead.
  • High-traffic interiors need washable eggshell and satin, not the flat builder paint most of these houses carry.
  • Decades of patch-and-repaint have left layered, uneven surfaces that need real preparation to look right.

Chautauqua & Baseline · 80302

The homes along the foothills edge near the park, including some of the most exposed property in the city.

  • The most aggressive ultraviolet load and the widest daily temperature swings of any Boulder neighborhood.
  • Wildfire-aware material choices matter at this interface, and some properties carry additional review.
  • Cedar, heavy timber and stone combinations call for stain systems rather than straight paint.

Wonderland Lake & north Boulder · 80304

The newer north end, where townhomes and contemporary builds meet the open space boundary.

  • Townhome and attached product comes with association color standards and shared-wall containment requirements.
  • Open-space wind carries dust across these lots more than anywhere else inside the city limits.
  • Contemporary elevations with large flat surfaces show application quality unforgivingly, so technique matters.

Questions

Neighborhood questions

Which Boulder neighborhoods do you paint?
All of them. Mapleton Hill, Whittier, Newlands, Table Mesa, Martin Acres, University Hill, Chautauqua and the north Boulder neighborhoods around Wonderland Lake, plus Gunbarrel on the northeast side.
Do Boulder neighborhoods require color approval?
The historic districts do, through Landmarks review. Several townhome and condo associations have their own standards. Most single-family neighborhoods outside those do not.
Are older Boulder homes different to paint?
Substantially. Pre-1978 construction brings federally required lead-safe work practices, and the trim detail on Boulder's Victorians and bungalows takes several times the labor per wall that modern construction does.
Do you paint student rentals on the Hill?
Yes, and that work books out early. The academic lease cycle concentrates turnover into a narrow summer window across the whole neighborhood.
