Boulder painting, by the numbers.

The weather window, the age of the houses and what published guides say painting costs, each figure tied to the agency or publisher it came from. No round numbers invented to fill a chart.

Direct answer: At the Boulder weather station the median last spring freeze is May 6 and the median first fall freeze is October 5 (NOAA 1991–2020 normals), and the average overnight low clears 50°F only in June, July and August. 55.4% of homes in the city of Boulder were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates), so lead-safe preparation is the normal case here, not the exception. Third-party cost guides put Boulder house painting at roughly $1.30 to $3.10 per square foot (HomeBlue, 2022), and painting a kitchen’s cabinets at $2,500 to $6,500 nationally (Fixr, 2026).

1. The month-by-month painting window

Exterior coatings care about the overnight low more than the afternoon high, because the film keeps curing after the crew leaves. Sherwin-Williams, for example, rates Duration Exterior for application down to 35°F and tells painters not to apply it when the temperature may fall below 35°F within 48 hours. Many standard lines and most stains, caulks and primers still ask for 50°F. Set those two lines against Boulder’s own normals and the calendar draws itself.

What the chart says in plain terms: April averages a 34.5°F low, so April exterior work is a day-by-day gamble. May (42.7°F) opens the season for low-temperature products. Only June, July and August average above 50°F overnight, which is the real window for anything that needs it. September’s 48.1°F average low is why September feels perfect in the afternoon and still needs product chosen for cool nights. By October the average low is 37.2°F.

2. When the freezes actually come

NOAA also publishes freeze probabilities for the station. They are more useful than an average date, because they tell you how much risk you are buying when you book early or late.

The longer local record agrees. NOAA’s Physical Sciences Laboratory, which sits in Boulder, keeps a frost record for the city going back decades: across 1948 to 2025 the average last spring frost is May 5, the latest was June 3, 1951, and the average first fall frost since 1948 is October 6, with the earliest on September 8, 2020. Those are the two dates to remember when someone offers to start your exterior in mid-April or finish it at Halloween.

Elevation moves all of this. The Boulder station sits at about 5,484 feet. Nederland is near 8,200 feet and Ward above 9,000, and the season up there is weeks shorter at both ends. See season and altitude for how we plan around it.

3. How old the houses are, town by town

Housing age matters to a painter for two reasons. Homes built before 1978 fall under the federal lead rule whenever paid work disturbs painted surfaces, and older homes carry more trim, more layers and more wood that has been painted many times. The Census groups construction years by decade, so the cleanest cut it allows is “built before 1980”, which slightly overstates the pre-1978 count.

The split is stark. Erie and Superior are mostly built since 1990, so lead almost never comes up there. The city of Boulder, Gunbarrel and Nederland are more than half pre-1980, and the canyon towns are older still. Read more on painting a pre-1978 home.

4. Who lives there: renters and owners

More than half of the occupied homes in the city of Boulder are rented, the highest share in the county and well above the state. That is why so much of our Boulder calendar is lease-driven: move-out repaints, especially around University Hill in late summer.

See rental turnover painting for how that calendar works in practice.

5. What published guides say painting costs

We do not publish a price list, because two houses with the same floor area can need very different preparation. What we can do is show what independent cost guides report, name them, and date them.

Two honest caveats. The Boulder figures are 2022 data (HomeBlue, February 2022), the newest Boulder-specific guide we could find on October 25, 2026; Angi publishes no Boulder page, so the two Angi rows are Denver-area figures, added as a more recent regional check. The Boulder figures are from 2022, and labor and material costs have risen since, so treat them as a floor for planning rather than a 2026 quote. And per-square-foot pricing hides the variables that actually decide a Boulder estimate: how much failing paint has to come off, how much trim there is, whether the house is pre-1978, and how hard the site is to stage. What painting costs walks through those.

Straight answers

Questions about these figures

When can exteriors be painted in Boulder?
For most products, from mid-May through September, with early May and early October workable only with low-temperature products and a good forecast. NOAA normals for the Boulder station put the median last spring freeze on May 6 and the median first fall freeze on October 5, and average lows reach 50°F only in June, July and August.
How many Boulder homes are old enough for the lead rule?
The Census reports that 55.4% of housing units in the city of Boulder were built before 1980 (ACS 2020–2024). The federal rule covers homes built before 1978, so the true share is a little lower, but it is still more than half in the city and over 90% in Ward and Eldorado Springs.
Why not publish your own prices?
Because a per-square-foot number would be wrong for most houses. Preparation, trim, access and the age of the paint drive a Boulder estimate far more than floor area. We show what independent guides report, with their dates, and give you a written estimate for the actual house.
Where do the numbers come from?
Weather figures come from NOAA’s 1991–2020 climate normals for the Boulder station and the NOAA Physical Sciences Laboratory frost record. Housing figures come from the Census Bureau’s American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates. Cost ranges come from HomeBlue (2022) and Fixr (2026). Each source is listed with a link above.
