Older homes

Painting a house built before 1978.

More than half the homes in Boulder are old enough that federal lead rules shape how a painter scrapes, sands and cleans up. Here is what the rule says, in plain language, and what it changes about your project.

  • Applies to paid work on housing built before 1978
  • Triggered above 6 sq ft per room inside or 20 sq ft outside
  • Containment, no open-flame burning, no unshrouded power sanding
  • Cleaning verification before the crew leaves

Direct answer: If your home was built before 1978 and a contractor is paid to disturb more than 6 square feet of painted surface per room inside, or more than 20 square feet outside, the EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting rule applies. The firm must be EPA-certified, must give you the Renovate Right pamphlet before work starts, and must contain the work area, avoid prohibited methods such as open-flame burning, and verify cleanup. Unless a test shows the paint is lead-free, plan for those steps in the estimate and the schedule.

Why this comes up so often in Boulder County

Lead was banned from residential paint in 1978, so the rule follows the age of the house. The Census does not split out 1978 exactly, but it does report homes built before 1980, and in this county that is a lot of houses: 60,174 of them, or 41.6% of the housing stock.

The numbers line up with what you see on the street. Downtown Boulder, Mapleton Hill, Whittier, University Hill, Martin Acres and Table Mesa are almost entirely pre-1978. Old Town Longmont and the original grids in Louisville and Lafayette are too. Most of Erie and Superior is newer, though their older cores are not.

What the rule actually requires

The EPA rule is written for contractors, but you are the one living with the result, so it helps to know what a compliant job looks like.

Before work starts

  • The company must be an EPA-certified renovation firm, and a certified renovator must be assigned to the job. This applies to every firm, including one-person outfits.
  • You must receive the EPA pamphlet Renovate Right before the work begins.
  • The paint can be tested. If testing by a certified professional shows the surfaces you are disturbing are lead-free, the work practices are not required.

During the work

  • The work area is contained: plastic sheeting on floors and ground, openings sealed inside, and the area marked off.
  • Some methods are prohibited outright: open-flame burning or torching of paint, power sanding, grinding or blasting without a HEPA-shrouded tool, and heat guns at 1,100°F or above.
  • Dust is controlled with wet scraping and wet sanding where possible, and HEPA vacuums.

When the work is done

  • The area is cleaned and the cleanup is verified with a specific wipe test before the containment comes down.
  • The firm keeps records of the job for three years.

When it does not apply

  • Homes built in 1978 or later.
  • Small jobs: 6 square feet or less of painted surface per room inside, or 20 square feet or less outside. Window replacement and demolition of painted surfaces are covered regardless of size.
  • Work you do yourself on your own home. The rule covers paid work.

We are not a lead abatement company and do not claim to be. Abatement, meaning work designed to permanently remove lead hazards, is a separate licensed discipline. Repainting a pre-1978 home is renovation work, and lead-safe practices are how that is done correctly.

What it changes about your estimate

Lead-safe work takes longer and costs more than a quick scrape-and-paint, and an honest estimate says so. The time goes into containment setup and teardown, slower wet methods instead of dry power sanding, HEPA cleanup at the end of each day, and the final cleaning verification. On a 1900s house with a century of paint on the trim, it is a real share of the labor.

That is also the most common reason two bids on an old Boulder house are far apart. A bid that does not mention the year the house was built, containment or cleanup verification is either pricing a different job or planning to skip the rule.

AskWhat a good answer sounds like
Is your firm EPA lead-safe certified?Yes, with a firm certification you can look up in EPA’s search tool.
Will you test the paint first?An answer either way, with the reason. Testing can save time if the surfaces turn out lead-free.
How will you remove loose paint?Wet scraping and sanding, HEPA-shrouded tools if any power tools are used. Never a torch.
How do you contain the dust outside?Plastic on the ground extending out from the wall, weighted, with the edges managed in wind.

Questions

Lead and older-home questions

Does my 1975 house need lead-safe painting?
If a paid contractor will disturb more than 6 square feet of paint per room inside or 20 square feet outside, yes, unless a test by a certified professional shows the paint being disturbed is lead-free. The federal cutoff is homes built before 1978.
Can you just paint over old lead paint?
Painting over intact paint without disturbing it is not a renovation under the rule. The problem is that a durable repaint needs loose paint removed and surfaces sanded, and that is the disturbance the rule is written for. A good estimate explains which surfaces will be disturbed and how.
Does lead-safe work cost more?
Yes. Containment, wet methods, HEPA cleanup and cleaning verification add labor. How much depends on how much paint has to come off. The written estimate shows it rather than hiding it in a single number.
How many Boulder homes were built before 1980?
About 26,300 housing units in the city of Boulder, or 55.4% of the total, according to the Census Bureau’s American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates.
