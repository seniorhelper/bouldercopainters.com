Guide · wildfire areas

Painting a home in Boulder County’s wildfire zones

People ask us for “fire-resistant paint” every summer. The honest answer is that paint is not where wildfire protection lives. Here is what the county, the state forest service and the research say, and what a repaint can do to help.

  • Fire-retardant coatings: most lost their effect within a few months in IBHS outdoor tests
  • The first five feet around the house matter most
  • The bottom 6 inches of siding should be noncombustible
  • Repainting is not a code trigger; replacing siding can be

By Boulder CO Painters · Updated October 25, 2026

Direct answer: No paint or stain makes a house fireproof, and fire-retardant coatings lose most of their effect within months outdoors. In Boulder County, wildfire resistance comes from materials and the first five feet: a noncombustible strip at the base of the siding, ember-resistant vents, clean gutters and nothing wooden touching the walls. A repaint is a good time to add them.

Can paint or stain protect a house from wildfire?

Not in any way you should count on. There are two separate questions hiding in that one, and it helps to split them.

Fire-retardant coatings. Wildfire Partners, Boulder County’s mitigation program, reviewed the evidence in October 2025. IBHS tests of several leading brands on siding left outdoors for up to a year found most lost their fire resistance within a few months; by three months none beat untreated wood in any meaningful way. Gels help for a few hours while wet, then stop. The program’s advice is to spend the money on hardening the house and on defensible space.

Ordinary paint and stain. These protect wood from sun and water; they don’t change what the wall is made of. Painted cedar is still cedar. Fiber cement is noncombustible either way, though James Hardie notes that its fire resistance does not extend to applied paints, which can char. What a repaint does offer is timing: it’s the one time a crew works the whole perimeter at ground level with the walls cleaned and exposed.

How do houses actually catch fire here?

Mostly from embers, not from a wall of flame. Wildfire Partners says more homes ignite from embers than from any other cause, and that embers can travel a mile or more ahead of a fire. Its fact sheet describes the sequence: embers and larger firebrands shower the siding and pile up at the base of the wall, where leaves and pine needles also collect. Wind drives the embers into that debris, it ignites, and flames burning against combustible siding are likely to ignite the house.

So the guidance keeps returning to the base of the wall, vents, gutters, the space under the deck and anything wooden touching the house. The Colorado State Forest Service puts the area 0 to 5 feet from the home in Zone 1, and recommends 6 inches of vertical clearance between the ground and the siding, 1/8-inch metal mesh over attic, eave and foundation vents, and replacing combustible fencing or gates at least within 5 feet of the home.

This is not only a foothills problem. The Marshall Fire of December 30, 2021, was a wind-driven grass fire that reached suburban streets. Boulder County’s updated count was 1,084 homes destroyed: 550 in Louisville, 378 in Superior and 156 in unincorporated Boulder County.

The 6-inch check

Measure from the soil or gravel up to the bottom of your siding. Wildfire Partners wants at least 6 inches of noncombustible material there: exposed foundation, metal flashing, fiber cement trim, stone or stucco.

Rules

Which wildfire rules apply to a painting project?

It depends on where the house is and on whether anything is being replaced. Paint alone is not what the codes regulate. This tool sums up what the county and city documents say; it is a starting point, not a permit decision.

Outside the City of Boulder and unincorporated county land, each town sets its own building code. Colorado’s Wildfire Resiliency Code Board adopted a statewide model code on July 1, 2025, and local governments with wildland-urban interface areas must adopt codes that meet or exceed it, so town rules are changing. Ask your building department before replacing siding, a deck or a fence.

What does Boulder County’s code say about walls?

For permitted work in unincorporated Boulder County, exterior walls have to be built one of four ways: noncombustible materials, heavy timber or log construction, fire-retardant-treated wood labeled for exterior use, or ignition-resistant materials from the top of the foundation to the underside of the roof sheathing. Cementitious stucco, cementitious cladding and metal sheeting count as noncombustible. Walls within 6 inches of the ground, a deck or a roof must be noncombustible there, and the base of the wall gets metal flashing or 1/8-inch mesh that still lets the wall drain.

The rule that matters most to a homeowner planning exterior work is the replacement threshold. The county allows a one-time exception when less than 25% of the surface area of all exterior walls is affected. Replace 25% or more, and the entire wall surface has to meet new-construction standards, and the 5-foot strip around the house has to comply as well: a weed barrier with at least 2 inches of gravel or crushed rock, or concrete, asphalt or pavers.

So a few rotten boards swapped during a repaint is maintenance; re-siding whole elevations of an old cedar house in the west county is a code project. If that is where you are headed, our fiber cement siding painting page covers how those boards should be finished.

And inside the City of Boulder?

The city adopted the 2024 wildland-urban interface code with local amendments; it applies to building permits in the city’s WUI area as of August 1, 2025, and only to the permitted scope (the city’s example: a deck permit triggers only the deck rules). Boulder Reporting Lab reported that council’s March 2025 changes also require permits for new siding and window replacement. A repaint is on neither list. If the house is a landmark or sits in a historic district, color changes go through a different office; see our historic home painting guide.

What should a repaint include in a wildfire area?

What we check on every exterior in the foothills, canyons and grassland edges. Little of it costs much with a crew already on site.

  1. The bottom 6 inches. If wood, vinyl, pressboard or composite siding runs down to the soil, Wildfire Partners suggests three fixes: cut the siding back to expose the concrete foundation, overlay the bottom boards with metal flashing or fiber cement trim, or replace them with a noncombustible material. We paint whichever you choose to match, and we caulk the top edge so water cannot get behind it.
  2. Soft bottom boards. Usually the ones that sat in wet mulch. Fiber cement trim often beats patching wood that will rot again.
  3. Vents. Foundation and eave vents should have 1/8-inch metal mesh. We mask vents rather than paint across them, because paint bridging the mesh blocks airflow, and we tell you when a screen is torn or missing.
  4. Gutters. We clean them before painting fascia. The county requires noncombustible gutters in new work.
  5. Under the deck. Firewood, lumber, cushions and storage bins under a deck are exactly what Wildfire Partners’ Cut the Clutter action is about. They have to move for a stain job anyway.
  6. The fence where it meets the house. A wood fence attached to the wall is a fuse. Replacing the first five feet with metal or another noncombustible material is one of the county’s rebate actions, and a metal section can be painted to match.
  7. The 5-foot strip. Pull bark mulch and junipers back from the foundation before we arrive.

And on every job: rags soaked in oil-based stain get laid flat outside to dry or sealed in a metal can with water. Oily rags can heat up and ignite on their own as they cure.

Is there help paying for this?

Wildfire Partners offers home assessments in west Boulder County and parts of rural east county, and runs a yearly rebate of up to $500 for base-of-siding, fence, vent and similar fixes. The 2026 round has closed (details in the questions below).

If you want the wood on the rest of the house to last, our cedar siding staining page covers stain choice and timing, and how long stain lasts on wood siding has the federal service-life numbers.

What we won’t tell you

That any paint, stain or sealer makes a house fire-resistant. If a contractor sells a coating as wildfire protection, ask for independent test results after outdoor weathering.

Questions

Wildfire-area painting questions

Is there a fire-resistant paint for houses?
Fire-retardant coatings are sold, but the evidence is poor. In the IBHS outdoor tests Wildfire Partners summarized, most leading brands lost their fire resistance within a few months on siding left outside, and by three months none offered meaningful protection over untreated wood. Codes don’t treat them as a main defense, and we don’t sell them as one.
Does painting fiber cement siding make it combustible?
The siding stays noncombustible. James Hardie says its fiber cement will not ignite when exposed to a direct flame, and also that this fire resistance does not extend to applied paints or coatings, which can char. A thin acrylic film is rarely what decides whether a house ignites. What sits against the wall matters far more: bark mulch, leaves, a woodpile, a wood fence.
Do I need a permit to repaint my house in a wildfire zone?
Neither Boulder County’s ignition-resistant construction publication (B-37) nor the City of Boulder’s wildland-urban interface code page lists repainting as a trigger. Both tie their rules to permitted work such as replacing siding, decks, windows or roofs. If a repaint grows into replacing siding, that changes: in unincorporated Boulder County, replacing 25% or more of the exterior wall area means the whole wall surface has to meet new-construction standards. Landmarked homes in the city have their own paint review.
What is the first five feet, and why does it matter for painting?
The Colorado State Forest Service calls the area 0 to 5 feet from the house Zone 1, the area nearest the home. Wildfire Partners funds work there, and Boulder County’s code requires gravel or another noncombustible surface 5 feet out from the walls on certain projects. For a paint job it is also our work zone: ladders and drop cloths go there, so it is a natural time to pull bark mulch and junipers back and leave rock or bare soil.
Can you paint the metal flashing at the base of my siding?
Yes. Wildfire Partners’ Protect Home Base fact sheet suggests colored or paintable metal flashing to blend with combustible siding, and says to seal the top edge with caulk so water does not get behind it and rot the siding. We clean the flashing, prime it with a primer made for galvanized or bare metal, and topcoat it to match the body or trim color so it reads as a deliberate band rather than a patch.
Does staining my deck make it more fire resistant?
No. Stain protects wood from sun and water; it does not change whether wood burns. Boulder County’s code looks at the decking material, the framing and what is underneath: for new work, decks less than 4 feet off the ground must be enclosed with metal mesh with openings of 1/8 inch or less, and the space below kept clear of plants, debris and stored combustibles. Wildfire Partners’ Cut the Clutter action targets the same storage problem.
Is the Wildfire Partners rebate still open?
Not for 2026. Boulder County’s announcement set the 2026 application deadline at 5 p.m. on October 8, 2026, with work and claims due by October 29. The program offered up to $500 for six actions, including covering the bottom 6 inches of siding with noncombustible material and replacing the first five feet of fencing attached to the house. Watch wildfirepartners.org or call 303-441-1420 for the next round.
